**Juice** (also called **vig**) is the sportsbook’s cut: the extra probability baked into the prices so that two sides of a market add up to more than 100%. If you treat −110/−110 like a fair coin flip, you are already paying a fee you have not named.

This lesson is about seeing that fee before you call a number “value.”

## The hook: −110/−110 is not 50/50

A fair two-way market on a true coin flip would sit near **+100 / +100** (even money). Each side would imply about 50%. The book would not get paid for standing in the middle.

Retail boards rarely look like that. A standard side market is often **−110 / −110**. Each price looks “almost even.” Together they are not even. That gap is the juice.

## Core: implied probability and overround

American odds convert to implied probability like this:

- **Negative odds** (favorites): `risk / (risk + win)`. For −110, that is `110 / (110 + 100) ≈ 52.4%`.
- **Positive odds** (underdogs): `100 / (odds + 100)`. For +150, that is `100 / 250 = 40%`.

Do both sides of a −110 / −110 market:

| Side | Price | Implied probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | −110 | ≈ 52.4% |
| B | −110 | ≈ 52.4% |
| **Sum** | | **≈ 104.8%** |

That sum is the **overround** (sometimes discussed as hold). The market is selling about **104.8%** of probability on an event that can only add to 100%. The extra 4.8 percentage points is the room the book needs so that, if the public splits in an inconvenient way, the prices still have a fee inside them.

A true 50/50 coin, stripped of juice, is not a −110 ticket. If you bet −110 on a fair coin, you are paying a toll to be in the game.

You do not have to memorize the formula to use the idea. Ask: **do these two (or three) numbers add to more than 100%?** If yes, you are looking at a priced market, not a laboratory probability.

## What the fee is doing

Juice is not a moral failing and it is not free entertainment. It is how a market-maker gets paid to post a number, take two-way action, and manage risk.

That has a few practical consequences:

- **Break-even is higher than 50%.** At −110 you need to win about 52.4% of equal bets just to get back to flat, before you even talk about “edge.”
- **“Small” juice still compounds.** A season of −110 tickets is a lot of fee if you do not have a better number than the close.
- **The posted pair is the product.** You are not buying a 50% coin. You are buying a 52.4% coin on each side of a 104.8% board.

## Traps: where juice hides

### Alt lines

Move a spread or a total off the main number and the juice often gets *worse*, not better. An alternate that looks like a “safer” side can be a more expensive fee. Always convert the alt to implied probability and compare it with the main. If you cannot explain the extra juice, you are shopping a different product.

### Parlays

Parlays multiply prices. They also multiply the book’s edge if you are using the same juiced legs the board already sold you. A “boosted” parlay that still starts from −110 legs is not a fair coin machine with a coupon stapled on. Price each leg, then price the combo. If you cannot do that, you cannot know what the boost is buying.

### Boosts and promos

A boost can reduce juice. It can also move you onto a worse number, a worse market, or a parlay you would not have built. Read the boosted price as a *new* quote, convert it, and compare it with the unboosted board — including the close, if you care about CLV. A promo is not a personality. It is a different fee schedule.

### Three-way and derived markets

Moneylines with a tie, player props, and other derived books often carry **more** overround than the main spread. More outcomes, thinner limits, harder to hedge: more juice. If the main board is 104.8%, do not assume the prop board is.

## Know the fee before you say “value”

Value, in the only sense this site uses it, means **a better number than the market’s honest probability — after juice.** You cannot claim that if you have not measured the fee.

A checklist that does not require a model:

1. Convert both sides (or all outcomes) to implied probability.
2. Add them. That is the overround.
3. Ask what a no-vig split might look like (there are several methods; they disagree at the edges, and that is fine).
4. Only then talk about whether *your* number is interesting.
5. Remember CLV: the close will tell you later whether the market agreed you had a price.

None of those steps is a pick. They are how you stop calling a −110 coin flip “a good number” just because it is familiar.

## Close

Juice is the fee. Name it before you call a number value. −110 / −110 is a 104.8% market, not a fair toss. Alt lines, parlays, and boosts are other ways to pay, or occasionally to pay less — not exceptions to the rule.
