A line move looks decisive on a graphic: **opened here, sits there, closed over there.** The graphic does not tell you *why*. A move can be information (injury, rotation, weather, a book getting hit and other books following). It can be noise (a thin late market, a limit cut, one shop shading a number). **Do not assume “sharp” just because the number traveled.**

Sporty Media reads moves as context. We do not treat them as instructions.

## The path: open → current → close

Keep three timestamps, not one:

1. **Open.** The first widely posted number. Often the least tested. Useful as a baseline, dangerous as a personality.
2. **Current.** Where the board is *now*, and how it got there — jump or drift.
3. **Close.** The last number with real limits. This is the CLV benchmark. Late, tiny markets can still print a silly close; note that when it happens.

A move from open to current is a live story. A move from current to close is often a different story (more information, or just fewer dollars).

## Steam vs grind

**Steam** is a sudden, coordinated jump: several books move together, often in a short window. It *can* mean informed money. It can also mean a respected shop moved and everyone copied, or a rumor hit the group chats. Steam is a *speed* description. It is not a character reference.

**Grind** is a slow drift: a point here, a few cents of juice there, over hours. It can be public money piling up. It can be a booker walking a number toward a close they already expect. It can be news leaking in pieces (questionable, then out). Grind is easier to narrate and easier to overfit.

Neither word tells you who is right. They tell you what the tape *looked like*.

## Money vs news

Ask which engine is more likely:

- **News:** a pitcher scratched, a star listed out, wind at 20 knots, a rest/travel report everyone can read. The move should be explainable in a sentence a beat writer would recognize.
- **Money:** the number moves and the news is stale, absent, or already priced. Then you are guessing about *who* bet and *how hard* — unless you have actual handle data, which most readers do not.

If you cannot tell money from news, write that down. “Unknown” is a valid board note. “Sharps love this” is not, unless you have something better than a vibe.

## Checklist before you narrate a move

Use this in order. Skip a line if you do not know; do not invent it.

1. **Who moved, and how far?** One book or the market? A nickel of juice or a full point of spread?
2. **Did other books follow?** A lonely move can be a shade, a limit, or a glitch. A followed move is still not a pick — it is a *consensus shift*.
3. **Public vs price.** Is the popular side getting *worse* (public hammer) or *better* (someone is lifting the other number)? Reverse-line folklore lives here; treat it as a hypothesis, not a law.
4. **Confirmed news?** Source it. “Out” from the team is not the same as a forum screenshot.
5. **When in the cycle?** Open steam, midday grind, and late thin moves are different animals. Limits often drop late; a last-minute tick can be a $200 bet looking like a trend.
6. **What did juice do?** Sometimes the spread holds and the juice flips. That is still a move. It is also still a fee change. See [How juice works](../how-juice-works/).

## Traps

### Reverse line move myths

The folklore: the public is on Team A, the line moves toward Team B, therefore “sharps” are on B. Sometimes handle and price disagree. Sometimes the “public” number you saw is a TV graphic. Sometimes the book is balancing, shading, or moving off a bad open. **A reverse move is a prompt to ask questions, not a signal to bet the other side.**

### Limits

A market that will take $5,000 and a market that will take $50 are not the same market. If a shop cuts a number to a postcard limit, a “move” may be one ticket. Note the limit if you can see it. If you cannot, do not pretend the move had institutional size.

### Late thin moves

Sunday night, injury pending, books half-off the board: the last number can whip around. That whip is a poor CLV teacher and a worse narrator. Label late thin markets as late and thin.

### Copy-paste steam

If every shop moves because one shop moved, you may be looking at **follow**, not independent confirmation. Follow is still information (the market is now *there*). It is not three separate geniuses.

## Board notes explain why — they do not say “bet the move”

A board note is a short field report:

- What was the open, current, and (later) close?
- What is the best *explained* reason for the shift?
- What is still unknown?
- What would falsify the explanation (news walks back, books bounce the number, etc.)?

It should not end with a side. If you want the house rule in writing, read the [style guide](../../style-guide/). For the format we use on baseball and football cards, see the [MLB / NFL board notes template](../board-notes-mlb-nfl-template/).

## Close

A move is info or noise. Steam is speed. Grind is drift. Money is not automatically smarter than news, and news is not automatically fully priced. Use the checklist, distrust reverse-line myths and late thin ticks, and write down what you do not know. The job is to explain a number that shifted — not to bet the arrow.
